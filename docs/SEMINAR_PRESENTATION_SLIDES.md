### SLIDE 6: FORENSIC THREAT ANALYSIS & THE 5-STAGE ATTACK CHAIN
* **The Universal Cybercrime Attack Lifecycle:**
  1. *Reconnaissance:* Data scraping, public registry mining, VoIP spoofing.
  2. *Psychological Hook:* Terror of arrest, greed for 500% ROI, panic over electricity disconnection.
  3. *Technical Bypass:* Sideloaded APKs, cloned silicone thumbs, AnyDesk screen mirroring.
  4. *Asset Exfiltration:* High-velocity UPI/IMPS transfers to regional mule bank networks.
  5. *Laundering & Exit:* Conversion to crypto, hawala transfers, and severance of communication.
* **Key Finding:** Attackers succeed because technical exploitation is always preceded by psychological incapacitation.
* **Presenter Speaking Note (45s):**  
  *"Across all eight cases, we mapped a universal 5-stage attack chain. The critical takeaway for cybersecurity engineers is that technical defense must intervene before Stage 3—introducing cognitive speed-bumps and blocking remote mirroring before funds are transferred."*

### SLIDE 7: ETHICAL ANALYSIS & RESPONSIBLE TECHNOLOGY
* **The 4 Pillars of Digital Ethics:**
  * **Privacy by Design & Default:** Data minimization; no application should request contact lists or device storage for loan processing.
  * **Informed Consent Integrity:** Ban dark patterns, forced bundled permissions, and deceptive terms of service.
  * **Duty of Care to Vulnerable Populations:** Provide cognitive safety nets, vernacular audio confirmations, and friction for senior citizens.
  * **Algorithmic Integrity & Anti-Deception:** Ban unwatermarked dual-use generative AI models; enforce the C2PA provenance standard.
* **Presenter Speaking Note (45s):**  
  *"Ethics cannot be an afterthought. When a loan application demands access to your family photo album, or when public land portals expose citizen thumbprints online, that is a failure of digital ethics before it becomes a crime. Engineers must build Privacy by Design directly into architecture."*

### SLIDE 8: STATUTORY MAPPING: INDIAN CYBER LAWS
* **Information Technology Act, 2000:**
  * *Sec 66D:* Cheating by personation (Cases 1, 3, 4, 7, 8) — 3 yrs jail + fine.
  * *Sec 66C:* Identity theft of biometrics/tokens (Cases 2, 4, 6, 8) — 3 yrs jail + fine.
  * *Sec 66E:* Violation of privacy / private area images (Case 5) — 3 yrs jail or ₹2L fine.
  * *Sec 43 & 66:* Computer hacking and trojan introduction (Cases 2, 7) — 3 yrs jail or ₹5L fine.
* **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS):**
  * *Sec 318(4):* Cheating and dishonest property delivery (All Cases) — Up to 7 yrs rigorous jail.
  * *Sec 204:* Impersonating a public servant (Case 1) — Up to 3 yrs jail.
  * *Sec 308:* Extortion by fear of injury (Cases 1, 5) — Up to 3 yrs jail.
* **DPDP Act 2023 & RBI Directives:** Penalties up to ₹250 Crores for data breaches; Zero customer liability if reported within 3 days.
* **Presenter Speaking Note (60s):**  
  *"We have thoroughly mapped both the IT Act and the newly enacted Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Notice Section 318(4) of BNS, which replaces the classic IPC Section 420 for cheating, and BNS Section 204 for impersonating police officers. Furthermore, the DPDP Act 2023 imposes massive fines on entities failing to protect biometric and personal data."*
